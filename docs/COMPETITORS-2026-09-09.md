## Which competitors deserve most attention?

Prioritize PrizePool for scale and its content/reward loop, BOLD CAT/Pipi Chick/Gryffindor for the other million-install businesses, ARE Apps for consumable pricing, and Javier Fernandez for paid historical content without depending on face recognition. This is a research priority order, not an earnings ranking.

All four verified million-install Android listings disclose ads and IAP. This makes a hybrid model worth testing, but it does not prove it causes their scale or is profitable. A one-time pack alone has a revenue ceiling; repeat purchases need a reliable editorial pipeline. Ads require active daily users, consent/audience controls and enough delivered impressions. A subscription requires recurring value. None can compensate for poor retention.

## Proposed Leo offer

- Free: guest practice, daily challenge, hints/skips, explanations and local progress.
- First purchase experiment: a clearly scoped themed pack at £2.99, compared with a £4.99 variant after a meaningful sample and content-value interviews. Prices are hypotheses, not store configuration.
- Bundle later: £6.99 for several explicitly named existing packs. Do not promise all future content for a low lifetime price.
- Rewarded ads later: an optional extra clue/cosmetic reward, with a free path retained. No forced ad mid-question and no purchased scoring advantage.
- Subscription later only if recurring content and repeat demand are demonstrated.

Keep the initial beta ad-free and commerce disabled while editorial/device/purchase gates are incomplete. Before public monetization, choose launch audience/countries, validate the value of the current 40-question pack, configure native products and test restores/refunds/pending purchases. The current guest-only design does not promise purchases or progress moving between iOS and Android.

## How to validate revenue instead of guessing

Collect consistent 30/90-day, platform and country estimates for the exact package IDs from a licensed app-intelligence source, recording whether figures cover consumer spend, publisher proceeds or ads. Public store counters are cumulative installs, not monthly users. No paid research subscription or data purchase has been made here.

For Leo, measure consent-appropriate cohorts: first round completion, D1/D7/D30 retention, voluntary second rounds, purchase conversion, repeat purchases, net receipts, refunds and cost of acquiring users. Track content and support cost. Test pricing only after instrumentation and sufficient users exist; do not claim a statistically validated winner from a handful of purchases.
